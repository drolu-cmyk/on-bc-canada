Insight · Dr. Oluwabiyi Adeyemo · August 2026

The next AI skill gap is judgement.

As tools become easier to use, the advantage shifts to people who can frame the problem, test the output, see the risk and explain the decision.

AI adoption is becoming easier to start and harder to govern well. A capable tool can produce a polished answer in seconds. That does not tell us whether the question was framed correctly, the evidence is reliable or the result belongs in a real decision.

The practical skill gap is moving upstream. People need to know what problem they are solving, what data sits behind the system, where failure can occur and who remains accountable when the output is used.

This matters in Canadian workplaces because AI does not arrive alone. It touches cybersecurity, privacy, procurement, cloud architecture, records, risk and management responsibility. Treating it as a stand-alone software skill leaves too much of the real work outside the training.

That is why applied learning needs more than tool fluency. Learners need repeated practice making choices, testing evidence and explaining limitations. The goal is not to make every person an AI engineer. It is to make more people capable of recognizing when an AI-enabled decision is sound, weak or unsafe.

As models improve, this judgement becomes more valuable. Better technology does not remove the need for accountable people. It raises the standard for what those people need to understand.
